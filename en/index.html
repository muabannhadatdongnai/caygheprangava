Why replace a missing tooth

Tooth loss affects more than your smile

Left untreated, a missing tooth causes the jawbone underneath to gradually shrink, leading to a chain of problems:

  • Difficulty chewing, which can affect digestion
  • Neighboring teeth shifting, causing bite problems
  • Jawbone loss, leading to a sunken, aged appearance
  • Unclear speech and lower confidence when talking
The proven solution

Implants — trusted for more than 50 years

A dental implant replaces the missing tooth root with a biocompatible titanium post, topped with a porcelain crown. It is a more predictable, longer-lasting solution than a traditional bridge or root canal-supported crown.

Benefits of dental implants

Compared with a dental bridge or removable dentures, implants deliver better long-term chewing function and jawbone health.

Treatment process

How long does a dental implant take?

A standardized clinical process, personally overseen by our dedicated implant specialist from consultation through to the final crown.

1

Exam & CT scan

Assess jawbone volume and plan your treatment. About 30-45 minutes.

2

Implant placement

Under local anesthesia, the titanium post is placed into the jawbone — gentle and minimally invasive.

3

Osseointegration

2-4 months (lower jaw) or 4-6 months (upper jaw) for the implant to fully bond with the bone.

4

Final crown

The finished porcelain crown is fitted, with regular checkups to ensure long-term durability.

Is it right for you

Dental implants for special cases

Implants can be performed safely for most patients — including older adults or those with underlying conditions — once those conditions are well managed.

Seniors

Safe when overall health is stable and there is enough jawbone density; bone grafting can be added if needed.

Patients with diabetes

Still possible with well-controlled blood sugar (HbA1c under 7%), supporting proper healing.

Patients with high blood pressure

Safe when blood pressure is well managed with medication, with close monitoring during surgery.

Patients with bone loss

Bone grafting, sinus lifts, or All-on-4/6 techniques can be used to minimize the need for extra grafting.

Implants vs. dentures

Which one actually saves you more?

Upfront price isn't the only thing worth weighing — look at the long-term cost of ownership too.

Removable dentures

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Chewing force only 30-40% of a natural tooth
  • Does not prevent jawbone loss
  • Needs relining or replacement within a few years
  • Inconvenient daily removal and cleaning

Dental implants

  • Higher upfront investment
  • Chewing power close to 100% of a natural tooth
  • Effectively prevents jawbone loss
  • Lasts 15-20 years or longer, fewer repairs
  • Cared for like a natural tooth, no removal needed
